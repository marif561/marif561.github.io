01 / ABOUT

Data mining, language and images, in service of health.

My research sits where natural language processing, social data mining and medical imaging meet clinical need. At Yale I help build an LLM-based system for longitudinal phenotyping of behavioral health from clinical records, where I lead the behavioral ontology and annotation work that gives the models a reliable ground truth.

Across projects I care about the same questions: does a model transfer to a new domain, where does it fail and for whom, and can its decisions be explained to the people who rely on them? That has led to work on transformer transfer learning, domain adaptation, subgroup-level failure analysis and explainable AI for mental-health detection, radiology reporting and chest X-ray interpretation.

Before Yale I spent two years as adjunct faculty at Colorado State University Pueblo, where I designed and taught six computing courses. I now also mentor a growing international cohort of student research interns.
