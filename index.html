I am a postdoc at Yale University. My research focuses on making large language models reliable in tasks that require structured outputs, grounding in source evidence, and accurate evaluation of agent actions. I address these challenges through token-level preference optimization for structured generation, hypothesis-based retrieval, and reference-guided evaluation of agent tool calls, using clinical and financial text as primary testbeds. I also work on digital mental health and AI safety, biomedical image analysis, and multilingual NLP.

Previously, I was an Adjunct Professor of Computer Science at Colorado State University Pueblo. I received my PhD in Computer Science from Instituto Politécnico Nacional, Mexico, advised by Prof. Alexander Gelbukh and Prof. Grigori Sidorov.

I welcome research collaborations and conversations about faculty and research positions. Feel free to get in touch. My CV is available on request.

Research interests

  • AI evaluation in real-world use: frameworks and metrics for how AI tools perform in behavioral health, digital mental health and education products.
  • Digital mental health and AI safety: detecting depression and suicidal ideation, and building risk detection and safety escalation into human–AI conversations with vulnerable users.
  • Agentic AI: LLM agents and agentic systems, including agents that support research.
  • Measurement and annotation: ontologies, annotation guidelines and inter-rater agreement for language-based measures.
  • Large language models for clinical text: benchmarks, LLM-as-judge evaluation, preference optimization and retrieval-augmented generation.
  • Biomedical image analysis and multimodal AI: deep learning on neuroimaging, EEG, medical images, audio and clinical reports, with SHAP and Grad-CAM explanations.
  • Multilingual NLP: mental health, social support, hope speech and misinformation across languages, including Spanish.
