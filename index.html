01 / ABOUT

Language as evidence, AI as an intervention to be measured.

At Yale School of Medicine I work with Dr. Samah Fodeh-Jarad on machine learning and LLM methods for secure patient–clinician messages (the PVminer and EPPC-Care projects), on a behavioral ontology and annotation framework for longitudinal phenotyping, and on NIH grant development in digital mental health and AI safety. I train foundation and transformer models, apply preference optimization to structured information extraction, and build retrieval-augmented LLM applications.

My broader agenda treats AI systems the way intervention science treats a new treatment: define the constructs, validate the measures, design the evaluation and test whether results hold up. That has led to work on label leakage in mental-illness classification, LLM benchmarks for depression and suicidal ideation in Spanish, explainable multimodal models for autism screening and emotion recognition, and LLMs for radiology reporting.

I earned my Ph.D. in Computer Science with a perfect 10.00/10.00 CGPA at the Centro de Investigación en Computación (CIC), Instituto Politécnico Nacional, Mexico, under Prof. Alexander Gelbukh and Prof. Grigori Sidorov. Before Yale I was an Adjunct Professor at Colorado State University Pueblo, where I designed and taught six courses.
