I am a Postdoctoral Associate in the Department of Emergency Medicine at Yale School of Medicine. My research is on evaluating AI in real-world health use: how large language models and multimodal systems perform with real patients, clinicians and vulnerable users, and how to measure that reliably. I work on digital mental health and AI safety, NLP for secure patient–clinician messages (the PVminer and EPPC-Care projects), measurement and annotation frameworks, and multilingual mental-health benchmarks, including for Spanish-speaking populations.

I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science with Distinction from the Centro de Investigación en Computación (CIC), Instituto Politécnico Nacional, Mexico, advised by Prof. Alexander Gelbukh and Prof. Grigori Sidorov, with a thesis on multi-class and cross-lingual fake news detection. I hold an M.Eng. in Geographic Information Systems from Wuhan University. Before Yale, I was an Adjunct Professor of Computer Science at Colorado State University Pueblo, and a research intern at Penn State Abington and research scholar at the University of Colorado, Colorado Springs.

I am open to research collaborations and faculty and research positions. Feel free to reach out; my CV is available on request.

Research interests

  • AI evaluation in real-world use: frameworks and metrics for AI interventions in behavioral health, digital mental health and EdTech products.
  • Digital mental health and AI safety: depression and suicidal-ideation detection, and risk detection and safety escalation in human–AI interaction for vulnerable users.
  • Measurement validation: ontologies, annotation frameworks and inter-rater agreement for language-based measures.
  • Large language models: benchmarks, LLM-as-judge evaluation, preference optimization and retrieval-augmented generation for clinical text.
  • Multimodal and explainable AI: neuroimaging, EEG, images, audio and clinical reports, with SHAP and Grad-CAM explanations.
  • Multilingual and cross-cultural NLP: mental health, social support, hope speech and misinformation across languages.
